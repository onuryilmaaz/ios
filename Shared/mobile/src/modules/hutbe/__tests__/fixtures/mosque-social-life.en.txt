THE MOSQUE AND SOCIAL LIFE
Dear Muslims!
Mosques are sacred places that embody our
brotherhood, symbolize our independence, and
reflect our faith in daily life. They bring believers
together around the same faith and ideals and direct
them toward the same qiblah. Every mosque is a
compass that guides us toward the right path, a
symbol of tawhid, and a center of unity. It is a place
of fellowship and affection that strengthens our unity
and solidarity and brings our hearts closer together.
Dear Believers!
In Islamic civilization, mosques have never
been merely places of prayer; rather, they have stood
at the very heart of life. Our cities developed around
mosques, while madrasahs, libraries, marketplaces,
inns, bathhouses, and charitable foundations served
humanity in their vicinity. The divine command of
Allah the Almighty, “The believers, both men and
women, are guardians of one another. They
encourage good and forbid evil, establish prayer
and pay alms-tax, and obey Allah and His
Messenger…”1 began in the mosques and
permeated every sphere of social life. The joy of
weddings was shared beside mosques; the sorrow of
funerals was eased in mosques; the hopes of the
lonely and the needy blossomed around mosques;
and orphans and those without support found
protection in them.
Dear Brothers and Sisters!
Regrettably, rapid urbanization, growing
attachment to worldly concerns, and individualism
today are causing the values represented by mosques
to be forgotten. This situation creates distance
between hearts that share the same faith, weakens
social bonds, and leaves people isolated. Yet our
mosques are among the foremost places that can
bring us peace and tranquility and strengthen our
bonds of brotherhood. Indeed, the Prophet
Muhammad (saw) addressed the believers who
gathered in the mosque for prayer as follows: “Keep
your rows straight and do not stand apart, lest
your hearts become divided as well.”2
Honorable Believers!
Every year, we observe the first week of
October as “Mosques and Religious Officials
Week.” This year, in order to raise awareness and
revive the historical role of our mosques, we will
mark the week under the theme “The Mosque and
Social Life.” Through the programs to be organized
by our mufti offices and religious officials, we will
contribute to further strengthening the connection
between our mosques and social life. We will strive
to make our mosques vibrant centers of social life,
where our children develop regular habits of
worship, our young people form religious study
circles, our elders come together in their courtyards
for friendly conversation, and, in short, families
experience the joy of being together.
Dear Muslims!
We pray for Allah’s mercy and forgiveness
upon our religious officials who have contributed to
the spiritual development of our nation from the past
to the present, upon the benefactors who contributed
to the construction of our mosques, and upon the
members of our congregations who have passed on
to the Hereafter. We also pray for health and well-
being for those who are still with us.
